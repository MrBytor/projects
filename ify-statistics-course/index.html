Semester 01

Statistics

Weeks 1–12

Build the mathematical foundations, describe data, and use probability and statistical evidence to reach conclusions.

01Mathematical foundationsThe numbers and calculations behind statistics.
  • Sets, set operations and counting principles.
  • Arithmetic, order of operations and basic algebra.
  • Measurement and unit conversions.
  • Percentages, ratios and practical calculations.
02Introduction to statistical samplingWho do we study, and how do we choose them?
  • Populations and samples; parameters and statistics.
  • Simple random and systematic sampling.
  • Stratified and cluster sampling.
  • Sampling error, bias and evaluating a sampling method.
03Descriptive statisticsSummarise the centre, spread and shape of a dataset.
  • Mean, median and mode.
  • Range, quartiles and interquartile range.
  • Variance, standard deviation and coefficient of variation.
  • Interpreting skewness and kurtosis.
04Data presentation and frequency distributionsTurn raw data into useful tables and graphs.
  • Frequency tables and cumulative frequency distributions.
  • Mean, median and mode from frequency tables.
  • Histograms, bar charts and pie charts.
  • Box plots, stem-and-leaf plots, scatter plots and lines of best fit.
05Basic probability conceptsDescribe chance and calculate the likelihood of events.
  • Experiments, outcomes, sample spaces and events.
  • Venn diagrams, complements and probability rules.
  • Independent and mutually exclusive events.
  • Conditional probability and Bayes’ theorem.
  • Basic ideas of expectation and variance.
06Discrete probability distributionsModel outcomes that can be counted.
  • Discrete random variables and probability distributions.
  • Conditions for a binomial distribution.
  • Calculating binomial probabilities.
  • Mean, variance and standard deviation of a binomial distribution.
07Continuous probability distributionsUse the normal distribution to understand measured data.
  • Continuous random variables and probability density.
  • The shape and properties of the normal distribution.
  • The standard normal distribution and z-scores.
  • Finding probabilities and applying the normal model.
08Statistical inference and estimationUse a sample to estimate a population value.
  • Sampling distributions, standard error and the Central Limit Theorem.
  • Point estimates and confidence intervals.
  • Confidence levels and margin of error.
  • Using z-scores and t-scores in estimation.
09Hypothesis testingTest a claim using evidence from a sample.
  • Null and alternative hypotheses.
  • Significance levels and p-values.
  • One-sample z-tests and t-tests.
  • Type I and Type II errors; interpreting a test conclusion.
10Advanced hypothesis testingCompare two populations and test population variance.
  • Paired samples and independent samples.
  • Two-sample tests for population means.
  • Choosing a test when population variances are known or unknown.
  • Chi-square tests for a population variance and interpreting results.
11Correlation and simple regressionExplore relationships and make predictions.
  • Scatter plots and Pearson’s correlation coefficient.
  • Simple linear regression and the least-squares method.
  • Interpreting regression coefficients and model assumptions.
  • Variation in regression: ANOVA and R².
  • What correlation and regression results can and cannot tell us.
12Revision and exam preparationBring the semester’s methods together.
  • Review sampling, descriptive statistics and data presentation.
  • Practise probability and probability distributions.
  • Revisit estimation, hypothesis testing and regression.
  • Work through mock exam questions and check solutions.

Semester 02

Project Skills

Weeks 13–24

Investigate a sustainability problem, work as a team, and communicate your research through a project and individual reflection.

13Introduction to the projectUnderstand the project and begin working as a team.
  • What a project is and how a research project develops.
  • The purpose and importance of project management.
  • Group working and initial project ideas.
  • Understanding the module’s project expectations.
14Wicked problems and teamworkExplore complex problems without a single simple solution.
  • Project-based and problem-based learning.
  • What makes a problem “wicked”.
  • Different approaches to sustainability problems.
  • Teamwork skills and barriers to working effectively together.
15Initiating a projectTurn a broad interest into a focused research question.
  • The project life cycle and the initiation stage.
  • Mind maps and feasibility studies.
  • Selecting and narrowing a sustainability topic.
  • Research questions and the research focus confirmation form.
16Planning a projectOrganise the research, tasks and timeline.
  • Using sources to refine the research focus.
  • Creating an annotated bibliography.
  • Breaking a project into manageable tasks.
  • Project flow diagrams, Gantt charts and planning tools.
17Research and literature reviewFind, evaluate and connect evidence.
  • Primary and secondary data.
  • The purpose and structure of a literature review.
  • Connecting research sources to your question.
  • Critical reading, skimming and other reading strategies.
18Developing the project artefactBring your research together into a project output.
  • Confirming the research topic and consolidating the research.
  • Working as a group on the project artefact.
  • Checking the academic content with your tutor.
  • Using feedback to improve the project.
19Group tutorialsReview progress and practise presenting your work.
  • Reviewing the group’s progress with tutor support.
  • Identifying the next improvements to the project.
  • Practising the group presentation.
  • Reviewing presentation and communication criteria.
20Presenting informationMake your evidence clear and easy to understand.
  • Features of an effective academic poster.
  • Organising information with useful headings and captions.
  • Finding relevant graphs and statistics for the project.
  • Explaining visual evidence clearly to an audience.
21Presentation skillsDevelop a clear, confident presentation.
  • Recognising effective presentation techniques.
  • Structure, signposting and speaking pace.
  • Using notes and visual aids effectively.
  • Practising delivery and avoiding common mistakes.
22Assessment review and workshopsCheck your work against the assessment requirements.
  • Reviewing the group project brief and marking criteria.
  • Reviewing the individual sustainability portfolio or blog.
  • Giving and using peer feedback.
  • Planning and making improvements to both assessments.
23Assessment tutorialsRefine the portfolio and resolve remaining questions.
  • Reviewing the portfolio or blog requirements.
  • Checking structure, content and marking criteria.
  • Improving work with individual tutor guidance.
  • Completing the remaining assessment work.
24Mock presentationsRehearse the final presentation and use the feedback.
  • Delivering a mock group presentation.
  • Receiving final tutor feedback.
  • Identifying improvements before the final assessment.
  • Clarifying final questions about the project and portfolio.

Outline based on the IFY Statistics with Project Skills Semester 1 and Semester 2 teaching materials. Weekly content may be adapted in class.
