Sampling and collecting data

Start by identifying who and what we want to study. Compare ways to select a sample and recognise bias, then bring these ideas together to investigate a canteen survey and judge what its evidence can support.

Class A 90 minutes

Populations, samples and variables

Learning objectives ↓

Identify the group being studied, distinguish sample statistics from population parameters, classify data and carry out simple random or systematic selection.

What this class covers

  • Population, sample and census; parameter versus statistic in relation to the stated research question.
  • Sample percentages as evidence about a wider population, with clear limits on what is known.
  • Categorical versus quantitative data; discrete counts versus continuous measurements; coded IDs and rounded measurements.
  • Simple random sampling from a complete list with distinct IDs; optional equal-individual-chance versus equal-sample-chance extension.
  • Systematic sampling: random start, interval N/n, sampling fraction n/N, selected IDs and reverse reasoning from later positions.
  • Repeating patterns in a sampling frame and differences between valid random samples.

Class B 90 minutes

Sampling methods and survey bias

Learning objectives ↓

Develop stratified and cluster sampling plans, distinguish chance variation from bias, and evaluate the reliability and practical cost of survey evidence.

What this class covers

  • Proportional stratified allocation, random selection within each group and fixed-total rounding; equal versus proportional group samples.
  • Cluster sampling of whole classes; comparison with stratified and simple random procedures.
  • Sampling variation versus bias; coverage/selection issues, nonresponse, leading questions and systematic measurement error.
  • Response rates versus support among respondents; optional logical bounds for unknown responses.
  • Accuracy versus precision using repeated-sample plots; why sample size does not repair biased procedures.
  • Select a defensible sampling method using cost, access and statistical limitations; evaluate and rewrite survey headlines.

Class C 90 minutes

Survey workshop

Learning objectives ↓

Investigate a canteen survey, check whether its claims follow from the data, repair its sampling and measurement methods, and write a defensible report.

What this class covers

  • Distinguish a reduction in portion size from a reduction in measured waste using a two-day pilot.
  • Calculate response rate, support among respondents and logical lower/upper support bounds among invitees.
  • Identify club-only coverage, leading wording and a miscalibrated scale; write a neutral survey question with balanced options.
  • Compare individual simple random selection with pair-based selection; reconstruct systematic sampling sequences.
  • Allocate proportional programme samples, interpret a realised cluster imbalance and diagnose programme-specific nonresponse.
  • Classify canteen variables, retain valid zeros, evaluate survey headlines and write a concise corrected report.
  • Seminar cases on changing population definitions, unequal sampling fractions, daytime-only contact, list periodicity and methods matched to study needs.
